RESEARCH NOTE · CONTEXT ENGINEERING · 26 OCTOBER 2026

Making context work harder.

A practical introduction to the approaches evaluated in my IEEE paper on production-scale multi-agent applications.

The problem: history keeps growing

Multi-agent workflows accumulate conversation history, tool outputs, and intermediate decisions. As that context grows, each subsequent model interaction can become more expensive and slower. The challenge is deciding which information must survive into the next step.

Three approaches to context management

1. Semantic summarization

Compress earlier interactions into a shorter representation of their meaning. A useful summary retains decisions, constraints, and unresolved questions so later agents can continue the work. The trade-off is that compression can lose important detail.

2. Relevance-based context editing

Select information according to the current task. Different agents and workflow stages need different context, so carrying every previous message into every interaction can be wasteful. Filtering needs to preserve dependencies that may not be obvious from the most recent request.

3. Multi-layer adaptive caching

Reuse results where repeated work would add cost without adding useful information. Caching complements summarization and editing: it targets redundancy, while the other two techniques manage what reaches the model.

What the evaluation reports

The paper evaluates these approaches in Adobe’s Project Stream across more than 10,000 real-world content generation requests over three months. It reports 60% lower token costs and a 40% improvement in end-to-end response latency against the baseline, with less than 3% output-quality degradation.

These are results from the paper’s evaluation, rather than a guarantee for other workloads. Context strategies should be assessed against the tasks, quality criteria, and operational constraints of each system.
